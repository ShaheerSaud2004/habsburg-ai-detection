To address these concerns, we convened a multidisciplinary expert panel to develop guiding principles and consensus recommendations for the development and use of trustworthy AI in future medical imaging. The panel identified four key principles for trustworthy AI in medical imaging: (1) beneficence, (2) non-maleficence, (3) respect for autonomy, and (4) justice. The panel also identified a number of specific recommendations in each of these areas. We believe that these principles and recommendations can serve as a foundation for further discussion and action on this important topic.

We study sum-of-squares (SoS) relaxations for the problem of graph coloring from the point of view of exactness. Given a graph $G = (V,E)$, the $k$-coloring problem is to find a map $f : V \rightarrow \{1,2,...,k\}$ such that no two adjacent vertices are mapped to the same color. We consider the problem of finding the largest $k$ such that $G$ admits a $k$-coloring, which is equivalent to finding the chromatic number $\chi(G)$ of $G$. The SoS hierarchy is a natural hierarchy of relaxations for $\chi(G)$, where the $k$-th level of the hierarchy is given by the following optimization problem: \begin{equation*} \max \sum_{i=1}^{k} x_i^2 \ \text{s.t.} \ \sum_{i=1}^{k} x_i \leq 1, \ x_i \geq 0 \ \forall i \in \{1,2,...,k\}, \ x_i x_j = 0 \ \forall \{i,j\} \in E. \end{equation*} The problem is to find the largest $k$ such that the $k$-th level of the hierarchy is exact, i.e. admits a solution that is also a solution to the original problem. In this paper, we show that the $k$-th level of the hierarchy is exact for all graphs of chromatic number at most $4$. Furthermore, we show that the $k$-th level of the hierarchy is not exact for any graph of chromatic number greater than $4$.
